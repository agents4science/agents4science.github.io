# Catalog Description (Winter 2027)

*Replacement text for the registrar catalog entry, drafted 2026-10-12 — send to the department curriculum coordinator.*

An AI agent is an autonomous program that perceives its environment, makes decisions, and takes actions to achieve specified goals. Large language models now make it practical to build agents that automate important tasks in science and engineering: synthesizing the literature, generating and testing hypotheses, writing and running analysis code, and operating computational and experimental facilities. This hands-on studio course teaches students to build, govern, and critically evaluate such agents. Lectures are brief; most class time is lab and studio work. Students build a working agent from scratch in the first session and extend it every week — adding tools, memory, and safeguards — through a series of shared challenges and a central project in which teams build agents that attempt to reproduce the computational results of published papers, or take on real tasks posed by client research groups at the University of Chicago and Argonne National Laboratory. All students work on course-provided infrastructure with individual computing budgets; studying how agents fail, and what they cost to run, is part of the curriculum. The course welcomes graduate students and advanced undergraduates from computer science, most interested in how to build such systems, and from the sciences, most interested in how to apply them. No prior agent experience is required; comfort with Python and the command line is assumed. See https://agents4science.github.io/2027 for details.
